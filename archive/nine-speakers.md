autonomous art devices. 9 of them.

each one is a speaker + a pi (or similar SBC) + a motion detector, physically
separate units scattered around a space. they can hear (mic in, or at least
sense each other's audio output) and sense motion nearby. they talk to each
other -- coordinate, react to what the others are doing/hearing/sensing.

the idea: they know they are an art installation. that's given, not a
mystery to the viewer. but THEY don't know where they are, or what "where"
even means for something like them. so they're using what they've got --
audio and motion, from themselves and from each other -- to try to work it
out. build a sense of their space, their neighbors, maybe eventually a sense
of "self" as one of 9 nodes in a room. the output (sound, maybe light/motion
of their own) is them thinking out loud about that, not a fixed composition.

not scoped further than this yet -- what "learning about their space" cashes
out to technically (some kind of local SLAM-ish thing from audio+PIR only?
a simpler emergent-behavior/cellular-automata model where each node just
reacts to neighbors and the "learning" is more metaphorical? real
inter-node localization via audio timing?), how they physically network
(wifi mesh? each just has decent range for its own mic/speaker?), what the
actual sound design language is -- all open.
